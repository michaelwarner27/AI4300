On the CCC2 thermostat, error code **E1** indicates a **loss of communication** between the thermostat and the system power module boards. This error can manifest in two ways:
* **Total loss of communication:** Between the CCC 2 thermostat and *all* system power module boards, which causes the system to shut down.
* **Individual loss of communication:** Between the CCC 2 thermostat and an *individual* system power module board. In this case, the LED will display error code E1 and the specific zone number that lost communication.

**Likely causes include:**
* Two power module DIP switches being set to the same zone.
* Issues with the communication cable.
* A need to try direct DC from the battery.

To safely discharge an RV air conditioner capacitor, service technicians should use a **capacitor discharge tool** (also called a discharge stick) to remove the electrical current. 

**Important Safety Warnings:**
* **Do not use a VOM (Volt-Ohm Meter)** to discharge capacitors; modern VOMs have a high impedance that makes this process ineffective.
* **Do not short the terminals with a screwdriver.** This is not recommended as it can cause the capacitor to swell, explode, destroy the terminals via electric arc, or weld the screwdriver to the capacitor.
* Always assume a capacitor is charged and discharge it before servicing a unit.

**Discharge Methods:**
* **Capacitor Discharge Tool:** Use a commercial tool or an assembled tool consisting of a 2k 5-watt resistor connected to an insulated alligator clip and an insulated probe (optionally including an indicator lamp for visual confirmation). To use, attach the clip to the common terminal and hold the probe to the other terminals.
* **Resistor Method:** Alternatively, you can discharge the capacitor using a 20,000 ohm (approx. 3 watt) resistor or larger.
* **Volt Meter Method:** You may discharge capacitors with a standard volt meter if you use a scale over 500 volts and touch the leads to each side of the capacitor.

---
