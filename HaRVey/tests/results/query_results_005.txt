On the CCC2 thermostat, error code **E1** indicates a **loss of communication** between the thermostat and the system power module boards. This error causes the system to shut down.

The likely causes for this error include:
*   **Loss of communication between the thermostat and all system power module boards.**
*   **Loss of communication between the thermostat and an individual system power module board** (in this case, the LED will display the error code and the zone number that lost communication).
*   **Two power module DIP switches set to the same zone.**
*   **Issues with the communication cable.**
*   **Power issues**, with a suggestion to try direct DC from the battery.

---

Sources:

Winterizing procedures vary depending on whether your plumbing system is equipped with a bypass kit and how you intend to winterize.

**If your system has a bypass kit:**
* Use the kit to close off the water heater.
* Drain the water heater completely.
* Leave the water heater closed off in the bypass position, especially if you are adding antifreeze to the plumbing system.

**If your system does NOT have a bypass kit and you are adding antifreeze:**
* Remove the anode rod and store it for the winter.
* Replace the anode rod with a 3/4" drain plug.

**General Draining and Storage Instructions:**
If the RV is being stored during winter months, the water heater must be drained to prevent freezing damage.
1. Turn off electrical power to the water heater (via the element switch or a breaker).
2. Shut off the gas supply.
3. Turn off the pressure pump on the water system.
4. Open both the hot and cold water faucets.
5. Remove the anode rod from the tank.
6. Follow the RV manufacturer's instructions for draining the entire water system.

**Warning:** Before re-lighting or turning on electrical power, ensure you refill the water heater with water and remove all air from the tank and lines.

To safely discharge an RV air conditioner capacitor, service technicians should use a **capacitor discharge tool** (also known as a discharge stick) to remove the electrical current and maintain a safe working environment.

**Important Safety Information:**
* **Do not** attempt to discharge capacitors using a VOM (Volt-Ohm Meter), as modern VOMs have a high impedance that makes this ineffective.
* **Do not** short the terminals with a screwdriver; this is not recommended as it can cause the capacitor to swell, explode, or destroy the terminals via an electric arc.
* Always assume a capacitor is charged and discharge it before servicing the unit.

**Discharge Methods:**
* **Capacitor Discharge Tool:** Use a commercial tool or a custom-assembled tool (using a 2k 5-watt resistor, an insulated alligator clip, and an insulated probe) to remove the charge.
* **Resistor Method:** One procedure involves discharging the capacitor with a 20,000 ohm (approx. 3 watt) resistor or larger.
* **Voltmeter Method:** You may discharge capacitors with a standard voltmeter if you use a scale over 500 volts and touch the leads to each side of the capacitor.
