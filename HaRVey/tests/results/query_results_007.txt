To safely discharge an RV air conditioner capacitor, you should use a dedicated capacitor discharge tool (sometimes called a discharge stick) to remove the electrical current.

**Important Safety Information:**
* **Do not** use a screwdriver to short the terminals; this is not recommended as it can cause the capacitor to swell, explode, or destroy the terminals via an electric arc.
* **Do not** rely on a modern VOM (Volt-Ohm Meter) for discharging, as their high impedance makes the process ineffective.

**Discharge Methods:**
* **Recommended:** Use a commercial capacitor discharge tool.
* **Alternative (DIY Tool):** You can assemble a tool using a 2k 5-watt resistor connected to an insulated alligator clip at one end and an insulated probe at the other. Attaching the clip to the common terminal and holding the probe to the other terminals will bleed the charge. Adding a 120 VAC indicator lamp can provide visual confirmation that the charge is being removed.
* **Manual Procedure (per Coleman-Mechanical-controls):** Disconnect all electrical power, then discharge the capacitor using a 20,000 ohm (approx. 3 watt) resistor or larger. Alternatively, a standard voltmeter can be used if the scale is set over 500 volts and the leads are touched to each side of the capacitor.

No, shorting the terminals with a screwdriver is not recommended. 

While some technicians do this to remove the charge, the procedure can cause the capacitor to swell or even explode due to the electrolyte being heated by the rapid discharge of electricity. Additionally, the electric arc can destroy the terminals, and the screwdriver can become welded to the capacitor. Repeatedly discharging a capacitor by shorting its terminals will destroy a good capacitor.
